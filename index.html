Interpretive standard — Oxford Dictionaries / OED & statistics terms click to expand
racist (n. & adj., Oxford / OED sense used on this site): A person who shows or feels discrimination or prejudice against people of other races, or who believes that a particular race is superior to another; showing or feeling such discrimination or prejudice.
sexism (n., ordinary dictionary sense): Prejudice, stereotyping, or discrimination, typically against women, on the basis of sex.
discrimination (n.): The unjust or prejudicial treatment of different categories of people, especially on the grounds of race, age, sex, or disability.

Map markers and score bars record reported claims and public record. This site does not adjudicate whether any event meets a dictionary definition — it surfaces contemporaneous reporting and official records so readers can judge.

Controversy themes (map triangles)

  • Race-relations — Prejudice, stereotyping, or hierarchy language about race or ethnicity (OED “racist” lens).
  • MaoriTreaty — Remarks or actions framed in public debate as hostile, dismissive, or discriminatory toward Māori or Te Tiriti / Treaty settlements and policy.
  • WomenSexism — Remarks or conduct treating women as inferior, or reducing women to a demeaning political “problem” (dictionary “sexism”).
  • QueerRainbow — Remarks or high-profile positions publicly framed as punching down on LGBTQI+ people or rights.
  • Climate — Climate-science denial or equivalent high-profile dismissal of climate risk in political speech or portfolio conduct.
  • Crime — Documented criminal convictions or court findings on the public record (not mere allegation or charge alone).
  • Other — Controversies in mainstream coverage that do not fit the theme buckets above.
  • Bill — A bill linked to a member or party on the timeline, with passed or defeated where recorded.

Sensitivity scores (0–100 bars on cards & party pages)

  • Rainbow — Heuristic of how supportive vs hostile the public record (House speech + news) looks toward rainbow communities. Higher ≈ more supportive in this snapshot.
  • Race-relations — Heuristic of race-relations sensitivity. Higher ≈ more progressive / less hostile in the snapshot.
  • Climate — Heuristic of climate-policy / science alignment in the public record. Higher ≈ more climate-aligned.
  • Women’s equality — Heuristic of women’s-equality sensitivity in speech and coverage. Higher ≈ more equality-aligned.
  • Immigrant — Heuristic of immigration / migrant-community sensitivity. Higher ≈ more migrant-supportive in the snapshot.
  • Colour scale — Fill is red at 0, yellow at 50; green appears only above 50 through 100. Tick marks at 25 / 50 / 75.
  • How built — Drawn from parliamentary record and news about that person in the snapshot dataset — not a poll, not an official ranking.

Party Stats & left–right

  • Party averages — Mean of member sensitivity heuristics for everyone included by the current filter (active only, selected year, election term, or all-time).
  • Left–right (0–100) — Site heuristic of overall ideological placement (0 = left, 100 = right). Not an official party ideology score.
  • Member count — Number of people in the party cohort used for that average under the active filter.
  • Controversy tallies — Counts of themed events for members in scope, only while they were in that party and inside the filter’s year window.

Map symbols

  • Solid square — Joined a party / entered Parliament on that line.
  • Empty square — Left a party or left Parliament.
  • Circle — Other station on the line (e.g. role marker).
  • Dotted path — Party switch (colour fades from previous party to new party); hover shows who moved where.
  • Orange / themed triangle — Controversy, crime, or related marker above the line.
  • Orange numbered bubble — Overlapping controversies / crimes / bills grouped by proximity.
  • Indigo numbered bubble — Overlapping MP name labels; open list shows ■ join, □ leave, or ● role for each person.

Attribution is only to the speaker or actor, not to those who called them out. Prefer Hansard and multi-outlet citations where available. Use Sources & fairness for outlet lean indicators.
